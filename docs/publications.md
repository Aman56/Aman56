# Research Publications

My research spans mechanistic interpretability, personalization systems, and production-scale machine learning. Below is a curated selection of publications organized by research theme.

## Featured Publications

### Mechanistic Interpretability

<div class="pub-item featured">
  <span class="pub-title">A Tale of Two Circuits: Grokking as Competition of Sparse and Dense Subnetworks <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11873" class="pub-link">[Paper]</a></span>
  <span class="pub-authors">William Merrill, Nikolaos Tsilivis, **Aman Shukla**</span>
  <span class="pub-venue">ICLR 2024 | Contribution: Co-author</span>
  <span class="pub-desc">This work investigates the mechanistic basis of grokking—a phenomenon where neural networks exhibit sharp transitions from overfitting to generalization. We identify that grokking emerges from a competition between sparse, well-generalizing circuits and dense, overfitting subnetworks. By analyzing learned representations at scale, we provide new insights into how models learn generalizable features.</span>
  <span class="pub-impact">Accepted at ICLR 2024 after initial preprint (2023). Received significant community engagement in mechanistic interpretability research.</span>
</div>

### Personalization and User Behavior Modeling

<div class="pub-item featured">
  <span class="pub-title">Modeling User Behavior from Adaptive Surveys with Supplemental Context <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20919" class="pub-link">[Paper]</a></span>
  <span class="pub-authors">**Aman Shukla**, David P. Scantlebury, Ritesh Kumar</span>
  <span class="pub-venue">Preprint 2025 | Contribution: Lead author <span class="pub-best">🏆 Best Paper</span></span>
  <span class="pub-desc">We propose a framework for synthesizing high-fidelity synthetic user respondents by combining adaptive survey responses with supplemental contextual behavioral signals. This approach bridges the gap between traditional survey data and implicit behavioral signals, enabling more accurate and nuanced user behavior modeling. The method has direct applications to audience understanding, market research, and personalization systems at scale.</span>
  <span class="pub-impact">Lead author. Recognized as Best Paper. Work conducted at Resonate Networks with production validation on real audience data.</span>
</div>

## Research Themes

My research bridges three core areas:

1. **Mechanistic Interpretability**: Understanding how neural networks learn and generalize at a fine-grained level. Focus on sparse circuit emergence, grokking phenomena, and feature representation learning.

2. **Personalization at Scale**: Building systems that model user behavior from diverse signals (surveys, behavioral data, contextual information) to enable personalized experiences. Work includes synthetic respondent generation and adaptive modeling.

3. **Production ML Systems**: Designing, deploying, and optimizing machine learning systems for high-scale inference and training. Experience with distributed systems, model serving, and large-scale data pipelines.
